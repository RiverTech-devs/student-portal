# Chronicle Hall (Social Studies) — Lessons to Review (70)

All 70 lessons are newly AI-authored (Social Studies had no game before). Each is a concept lesson (steps + worked example + misconceptions) with a randomized multiple-choice generator. Review for factual/historical accuracy, grade-appropriateness, and neutrality on civics/current-events topics. Highest-scrutiny: US/world history dates & causation, civics (Constitution/Bill of Rights/branches), and economics concepts where a distractor can be subtly wrong.

### Geography
 1. **Local Geography** _(grade K-2, stage 1)_ — describe the physical and human features of their local area and locate familiar places on a simple map
 2. **Reading Maps** _(grade K-2, stage 1)_ — locate places and follow directions on a simple map of a familiar area
 3. **Latitude & Longitude** _(grade 3-5, stage 2)_ — use lines of latitude and longitude to locate a place by its coordinates
 4. **Map Keys, Scale & Compass** _(grade 2-4, stage 2)_ — use a map key, scale bar, and compass rose to read symbols, distance, and direction on a map
 5. **Physical vs Political Maps** _(grade 3-5, stage 2)_ — distinguish physical from political maps and explain what each type of map shows
 6. **World Geography** _(grade 3-6, stage 2)_ — locate the continents, oceans, and major countries and describe the world's major physical regions
 7. **Human-Environment Interaction** _(grade 4-7, stage 3)_ — explain how people adapt to, modify, and depend on their physical environment
 8. **Migration** _(grade 5-8, stage 3)_ — describe why people migrate and how migration changes both origin and destination places
 9. **Regions** _(grade 3-6, stage 3)_ — group places into regions based on shared physical or human characteristics
10. **Geopolitics** _(grade 9-12, stage 4)_ — analyze how geography, resources, and power shape relationships and conflicts among nations

### US History
11. **Local History** _(grade K-2, stage 1)_ — describe how their local community has changed over time using stories, artifacts, and firsthand accounts
12. **American Revolution** _(grade 5-9, stage 3)_ — explain the causes of the American Revolution and how the colonies won their independence
13. **Colonization of America** _(grade 5-8, stage 3)_ — explain why Europeans colonized the Americas and describe daily life in the early colonies
14. **Constitution & Founding** _(grade 6-9, stage 3)_ — describe how the US Constitution was created and the principles the new government was founded on
15. **Native Peoples & Pre-Columbian America** _(grade 5-8, stage 3)_ — describe the diverse Native American cultures that existed in the Americas before European contact
16. **US History** _(grade 5-8, stage 3)_ — outline the major periods of United States history and place key events in chronological order
17. **Westward Expansion** _(grade 5-9, stage 3)_ — describe US westward expansion and its effects on settlers and Native peoples
18. **20th-Century US History** _(grade 8-11, stage 4)_ — outline major 20th-century US events, including the World Wars, the Great Depression, and the postwar era
19. **Civil Rights Movement** _(grade 8-11, stage 4)_ — explain the goals, key events, and outcomes of the Civil Rights Movement
20. **Civil War & Reconstruction** _(grade 6-10, stage 4)_ — explain the causes of the Civil War and the goals and outcomes of Reconstruction
21. **Industrialization** _(grade 7-10, stage 4)_ — describe how industrialization transformed the US economy, cities, and workforce

### World History
22. **Early Civilizations** _(grade 3-5, stage 2)_ — describe key features of early human civilizations, including agriculture, settlements, and early government
23. **Age of Exploration** _(grade 6-9, stage 3)_ — describe the motives and consequences of European overseas exploration and the resulting global exchange
24. **Classical Empires** _(grade 5-8, stage 3)_ — describe major classical empires such as Greece, Rome, and Han China and their lasting contributions
25. **Middle Ages** _(grade 6-9, stage 3)_ — describe life, power, and religion in the medieval world across several regions
26. **Renaissance** _(grade 6-9, stage 3)_ — explain how the Renaissance renewed art, learning, and science in Europe
27. **World History Overview** _(grade 5-8, stage 3)_ — outline the broad sequence of major world eras and civilizations from ancient times to the present
28. **Modern World** _(grade 9-12, stage 4)_ — describe major developments in the world since 1945, including the Cold War and globalization
29. **Revolutions** _(grade 8-11, stage 4)_ — explain the causes and effects of major political and industrial revolutions
30. **World Wars** _(grade 8-11, stage 4)_ — explain the causes, course, and consequences of the two World Wars

### Civics & Government
31. **Citizenship & Responsibility** _(grade 3-6, stage 2)_ — describe the rights and responsibilities of a good citizen and give examples of responsible civic behavior
32. **National Symbols & Identity** _(grade 1-3, stage 2)_ — identify major national symbols, holidays, and pledges and explain what they represent
33. **Bill of Rights** _(grade 6-9, stage 3)_ — identify the rights protected by the Bill of Rights and give an everyday example of each
34. **Federalism & Levels of Government** _(grade 6-9, stage 3)_ — distinguish local, state, and federal government and explain how powers are divided among them
35. **Government Basics** _(grade 4-8, stage 3)_ — explain the basic purpose of government and identify the main functions it performs for society
36. **The Constitution** _(grade 6-9, stage 3)_ — explain the structure and purpose of the US Constitution and how it organizes and limits government
37. **Three Branches of Government** _(grade 5-9, stage 3)_ — name the three branches of government and describe the role and powers of each
38. **Types of Government** _(grade 6-9, stage 3)_ — compare types of government such as democracy, monarchy, and dictatorship
39. **Voting & Elections** _(grade 5-9, stage 3)_ — explain how elections work and why voting is central to a democracy
40. **Law & Justice** _(grade 8-12, stage 4)_ — explain how laws are made and applied and evaluate a case in terms of rights, fairness, and justice
41. **Political Systems** _(grade 7-10, stage 4)_ — compare major political systems and explain how power is organized and exercised in each
42. **Civic Engagement** _(grade 8-12, stage 5)_ — plan and carry out an informed civic action that addresses a real community or public issue
43. **Leadership & Influence** _(grade 9-12, stage 5)_ — evaluate how leaders influence groups and demonstrate leadership by guiding a collaborative effort

### Economics
44. **Needs vs Wants** _(grade K-2, stage 1)_ — distinguish between needs and wants and sort everyday examples of each
45. **Goods & Services** _(grade 1-4, stage 2)_ — distinguish goods from services and identify who produces and who consumes them
46. **Money & Trade** _(grade 2-5, stage 2)_ — explain how money makes trade easier than barter and how people exchange goods and services
47. **Economics Basics** _(grade 4-8, stage 3)_ — explain how people meet needs and wants through producing, exchanging, and consuming goods and services
48. **Personal Finance & Budgeting** _(grade 5-9, stage 3)_ — build a simple budget and explain saving, spending, and the cost of borrowing
49. **Scarcity & Choice** _(grade 4-8, stage 3)_ — explain scarcity and describe the trade-offs and opportunity cost of a choice
50. **Supply & Demand** _(grade 5-9, stage 3)_ — explain how supply and demand interact to set the price of a good
51. **Markets** _(grade 7-10, stage 4)_ — explain how markets coordinate buyers and sellers and how competition affects prices and choices
52. **Advanced Economics** _(grade 9-12, stage 5)_ — apply economic models to analyze markets, policy, and trade-offs at national and global scales

### Culture & Society
53. **Community Awareness** _(grade K-2, stage 1)_ — describe their community, identify the people and places in it, and explain the roles community members play
54. **Diversity** _(grade 3-7, stage 2)_ — describe the diversity of people within a community and explain why respecting differences matters
55. **Traditions & Values** _(grade 3-6, stage 2)_ — describe traditions and shared values in a culture and compare them with their own
56. **Cultural Diffusion** _(grade 6-10, stage 3)_ — explain how ideas, goods, and customs spread between cultures through contact and exchange
57. **Cultural Studies** _(grade 6-10, stage 3)_ — describe the elements of a culture and compare beliefs, customs, and values across different cultures
58. **World Religions** _(grade 6-9, stage 3)_ — identify the major world religions and describe a core belief and practice of each
59. **Current Events Analysis** _(grade 6-10, stage 4)_ — analyze a current news event, identify its sources, and explain multiple perspectives on it
60. **Global Interactions** _(grade 6-9, stage 4)_ — explain how trade, communication, and migration connect nations and cultures around the world
61. **Modern Issues & Society** _(grade 8-11, stage 4)_ — analyze a contemporary social issue using historical context and multiple perspectives

### Historical Thinking Skills
62. **Chronology & Timelines** _(grade K-3, stage 1)_ — place events in order on a timeline and use terms like past, present, and future correctly
63. **Sequencing Events** _(grade 1-4, stage 2)_ — sequence a series of historical events in the correct order and describe what came before and after
64. **Cause & Effect in History** _(grade 4-7, stage 3)_ — identify the causes and effects of a historical event and explain how they are connected
65. **Continuity & Change** _(grade 6-10, stage 3)_ — identify what stayed the same and what changed across a period of history
66. **Historical Context** _(grade 6-9, stage 3)_ — explain how the context of a time period shaped the events and decisions within it
67. **Point of View & Bias** _(grade 5-9, stage 3)_ — identify the author's point of view in a source and explain how bias can shape an account
68. **Primary & Secondary Sources** _(grade 4-8, stage 3)_ — distinguish primary from secondary sources and use evidence from a source to answer a historical question
69. **Comparative History** _(grade 8-11, stage 4)_ — compare developments across two or more societies or eras and explain their similarities and differences
70. **Historical Analysis** _(grade 7-10, stage 4)_ — analyze a historical event using multiple sources, weighing evidence and perspective to build an interpretation
